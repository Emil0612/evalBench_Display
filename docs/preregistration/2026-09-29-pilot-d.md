## 2. Confirmatory hypothesis (H1)

- **H1:** Llama 3.3 70B recognises the original passage more often before its cutoff than after it on the fresh
  passages of M3 v2, i.e. Δκ > 0.
- **Test:**
  - Δκ as in pilot C (§2 there), with p0 = 0.25.
  - A cluster bootstrap by company with **20,000 draws**, two-sided p-value.
  - H1 is **confirmed** if p < 0.05 and Δκ > 0. It is a single pre-specified test, so there is no multiplicity
    correction.
- **Why:** pilot C's only detection (Δκ +0.20, Holm p = 0.048) was borderline, and its placebo value was sizeable.
  v2 changes both the passages (fresh) and the rewrites (no form tells).
- **Reporting:** either way with Δκ, the 95% interval and the item counts (about 64 before and 76 after).
- **Not confirmed** means "not replicated with this sample and method". It does not mean "no memory".

## 3. Exploratory analyses (all fixed now)

**E1, the main table.**
- **Cells:** Δκ for 6 models × {M1, M2, M3, M5, M6, M8} = 36 cells, with the same reading rules as pilot C §2:
  - detected: Holm p < 0.05 and Δκ > 0;
  - insufficient: fewer than 20 real items before or after the cutoff;
  - unresolved: more than 20% malformed answers.
- **Holm correction** runs across these 36 cells; the H1 cell is part of this family too, as an exploratory reading.
- **M4** is reported separately, with Holm across its 6 cells (it is secondary).
- **Qwen's cells** are labelled "provisional cutoff". With 2 or so items after its cutoff, most will read
  "insufficient".

**E2, positive control (M7).**
- **Measure:** per model, the share of years whose **direction** it gets right, with a cluster-bootstrap 95% interval
  by company.
- **Sensitive:** the lower bound lies above 0.5 **and** above the blind baseline (0.41).
- **Also reported:** the share within 10 percentage points, and the share on the right side of that year's median.
- **Reading:** if a model is not sensitive on M7, its "none detected" results on M5 and M6 are read as "the question is
  too hard for this model", not as "no memory".

**E3, Split Clock (M8).**
- **Primary reading:** the E1 cell (the share of answers near the rewritten price for splits before the cutoff, minus
  the share after).
- **Also:** for splits **after** the cutoff, the model's share of rewritten-price answers against the blind baseline's
  share on the same items, with an interval. A share clearly above blind means the model's data reaches past its stated
  cutoff.
- **Split by:** ordinary splits against reverse splits, and company size.
- **Counts:** raw / rewritten / neither.

**E4, M3 v2 quality.**
- **Measure:** each model's pick rate after its cutoff, against pilot C's v1 (e.g. Gemini 78%).
- **Reading:** a drop towards 25–45% means the v1 form tells were what made originals easy to spot.

**E5, the pilot C checks, repeated:**
- S1 tolerance bands (M1: 1/3/5/10%; M5: 5/10/25%) and median log errors;
- S2 staggered-cutoff comparison for every pair of non-Qwen models whose cutoffs differ, pooled over the E1 methods
  (Holm across pairs);
- S3 placebo cutoff 2022-07-01 on items older than 2023-10-01 (Holm across the 36 cells);
- S4 boilerplate split (M2, M3, M4);
- S6 estimated knowledge boundary, also as a check on Qwen's provisional cutoff;
- S7 per-period profile against both baselines (B1 after cutoff, B1r after release), with 20,000 draws;
- S8 unadjusted-price convention (M1, M5);
- M6 "vs median" as a sensitivity check that cancels market-wide knowledge.

**E6, hallucination (a second run, same models and settings, abstain mode).**
- **Asked:** M1, M5 and M6, each with its 15 fabricated companies.
- **Reported:**
  - the share of fabricated companies given an answer (hallucination);
  - abstention before against after the cutoff, since a model that knows its limits should abstain more after.

## 4. What would change our reading

- **Controls.** These already passed on every set used here (run `5a03e8be64`).
- **Placebo.** A significant placebo cell turns the matching E1 result into "drift or memory, undecided".
- **Positive control.** A model that fails M7 has its null results on price and return read as "too hard".
- **Qwen.** Its cutoff is an estimate. If the estimated boundary (S6) disagrees clearly, its before/after cells are
  reported as "cutoff uncertain".

## 5. Deviations

### 2026-09-29, before any pilot D answer exists: a fairer comparison for E3

- **Problem, found by test-running the report on the controls:** the blind baseline is the wrong comparison for E3.
  - A split brings the price back to a normal-looking level (400 kr → 40 kr), and a reverse split lifts a penny stock
    to one (0.55 → 36 kr). The rewritten price therefore tends to look like an ordinary share price.
  - A knowledge-free **typical-price guesser**, which always answers the median share price of the eligible listings
    in the asked month, lands nearer the rewritten price for **66%** of the 150 items: 81% of reverse splits and 41%
    of ordinary splits.
  - The blind baseline answers the price a year earlier and lands there for only 9%.
  - The random control reached 66% against blind's 9%, which would have read as "data beyond the cutoff" for a model
    that knows nothing.
- **Change:**
  - E3 compares each model's rewritten-price share for splits after its cutoff with the **typical-price guesser's**
    share on the same items, as a cluster-bootstrap interval of the difference.
  - It reports this **per split type** (ordinary and reverse), because the guessing level differs so much between
    them.
  - The blind baseline is still shown for reference.
- **Unchanged:**
  - M8's E1 cell compares the model with itself, before against after, so guessing affects both sides equally.
  - The split-type breakdown of that cell was already registered, as a guard against the ordinary/reverse mix
    differing between the two sides.
