# Analysis plan: M16/M17, US index memory as the reference for the Nordic result

**Date:** 2026-10-01. Committed before any model answered these sets. Changes after that go in §6, dated.

## 1. Why
- **The Nordic result:** the monthly-change test (M15) found market memory in four of five models, with correlations
  before the cutoff of +0.46 to +0.80.
- **The literature's reference:** GPT-4o recalls the S&P 500, Dow Jones and Nasdaq Composite almost exactly inside its
  training window (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu 2025), and these are among the most-written-about numbers in finance.
- **The question:** asking the same models the same questions about these indices shows how much weaker Nordic
  market memory is, and whether the models answer differently (finely graded, or a few stock values).

## 2. Sets and controls
- **Data:** daily closes from Yahoo Finance (`src/evalbench/fetch_us_indices.py`, `data/private/us_index_daily.parquet`).
  **PRIVATE:** never published, off the default views (private checks).
  - Checked against known published closes of each index.
- **Sets:**
  - **M16 `us_index_level/v1`** (deed3293) and **M17 `us_index_return/v1`** (8c573ff8);
  - 512 questions each: S&P 500 (SPX), Dow Jones (DJIA), Nasdaq Composite (COMP), Nasdaq-100 (NDX), every month
    2016-01..2026-08;
  - exactly M14/M15's wording and scoring (the same code), no pilot block.
- **Base rates:**

  | | Up months | Monthly change, SD | "Same month last year" level error (median) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Nordic | 62% | 4.3 pp | 10% |

  The US base rates are part of the private check and are not published here.

- **Controls:** oracle, random and blind were run on both sets before this plan; US results are a private check and
  are not published here. As in the Nordic set, the blind guesser does better on older months (the indices rose).
- **No micro-pilot:** the proxy evidence is strong. Every model recalled US share prices on M10, and the literature
  shows near-exact index recall.

## 3. Run
- **Models:** the six grid models, same pins and settings.
- **Size:** 512 × 2 × 6 = 6,144 calls.

## 4. Confirmatory tests
**Windows** (as the Nordic plan §6.3): BEFORE = every month up to the last whole month before cutoff − 91 days; AFTER =
every month after the release month.

1. **US market memory, per model (Holm across the five with an AFTER window, separately per method):**
   - **M17:** r_before − r_after, where r is the Pearson correlation between answers and true monthly changes over the
     answered questions; answers that never vary give r = 0. Cluster bootstrap by month, 20,000 draws, two-sided.
   - **M16:** on BEFORE months, the mean of model closeness − blind closeness, tested against 0 (cluster bootstrap by
     month).
2. **US vs Nordic, per model (Holm across all six models; this needs only BEFORE months):**
   - r_before(US, M17) − r_before(Nordic, M15) on the BEFORE months both sets share (the Nordic set without its 24
     pilot questions).
   - Paired cluster bootstrap by month: each resampled month brings its US and its Nordic questions.
   - Expected positive: index coverage in training data is far larger for US indices (Kandpal et al. 2023).

## 5. Exploratory (reported whatever they show)
- **E1, answer style per model and market:**
  - the number of distinct answers;
  - the share of the most repeated values;
  - the stock "don't know" answer: the most repeated value that does not track the market.
- **E2, per index:** level closeness and within ±5% before the cutoff (OMXS30 vs SPX, and so on). The level comparison
  is exploratory only: the blind guesser's difficulty differs between the markets, so model − blind is
  not comparable across them.
- **E3, the Market page:** the US path and the rolling correlation, Nordic and US curves side by side per model.

## 6. Deviations
### 6.1 (2026-10-01, after run 2df1965913 finished, before any US answer was examined): E4, "Nordic beyond US"
- **Why:** Nordic and US indices move together. A model that remembers only the US market's path (or the global story,
  "2022 fell") would still show a positive Nordic correlation on M15, so part of the Nordic result may be borrowed
  global knowledge rather than Nordic-specific memory.
- **Test, per model, on its BEFORE months** (the Nordic M15 questions without the 24 pilot questions):
  - US move of a month = the mean true monthly change of the four US indices that month;
  - partial correlation between the model's Nordic answer and the true Nordic change, controlling for that US move
    (both residualised on the US move by least squares over the BEFORE questions; answers that never vary give 0);
  - cluster bootstrap by month, 20,000 draws, two-sided against 0; Holm across the six models.
- **Reading:** clearly above 0 = the model knows something specifically Nordic; about 0 while the plain Nordic
  correlation is high = the Nordic result is mostly the shared global path.
- **Reported alongside (descriptive):** the correlation between the Nordic and US true changes on the same months; the
  same partial correlation on AFTER months.
- **Status:** exploratory (added after the run), but fixed before the US answers were looked at.
- **Run health:** 6,093 of 6,144 answers stored, 51 calls failed; the retry (same selection) goes before the report.

### 6.2 (2026-10-01, before the report): parser fix
- **Found on the Market page:** an answer of 500 for the S&P 500's monthly change. It was Gemini's plain-text refusal
  ("The S&P 500 index for November 2024 has not occurred yet"), read for the number in the index's name. Llama's reply
  cut off before its JSON was read as 31 (from "July 31").
- **Fix (as the Nordic plan §6, item 4):** only the JSON answer is read; broken JSON counts when the number follows the
  answer key; "I cannot answer" is a refusal. Stored replies re-read with `python -m evalbench.reparse`.
- **US sets:** 2 Gemini level readings (2, 500) and 2 change readings (Gemini 500, Llama 31) are no longer answers;
  13 Gemini change replies changed label only (malformed → refusal). The Llama reading was inside its BEFORE window,
  so a first-look correlation computed earlier predates the fix; the report uses the fixed readings.
